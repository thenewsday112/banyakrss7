Seabirds racing home over the ocean can get jet-lagged

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Flying across several time zones often causes jet lag, a feeling when the body insists it's the middle of the night while the clock says it's lunchtime. Now it turns out this nuisance is not exclusive to humans.

A new Science study on Manx shearwaters, seabirds that can fly hundreds of kilometers a day over the North Atlantic, shows they jet-lag themselves during their excursions. And because these birds navigate by the Sun, that jet lag leaves a measurable mark—it skews their compass.

Clockwork compass

Patrick Lewin is a behavioral and sensory ecologist at the University of Oxford and lead author of the study. “Manx shearwaters are medium-sized birds, about the same size as a pigeon,” Lewin said. “They are white underneath and black on top, but they have very long wings, and they are closely related to albatross.” Manx shearwaters nest in burrows on coastal islands around the North Atlantic. Most breed around the UK and Ireland, with about half of their population living in Wales.

The FDA wants to pull its approval of a drug. It’s not so easy.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

In May 2021, the US Food and Drug Administration released a lengthy briefing document on an experimental medication for a rare autoimmune form of vasculitis that often damages the kidneys and lungs. The agency was on the fence about whether to approve the treatment, and it wanted an independent advisory committee to weigh in. Among the questions at hand: Was the drug meaningfully better than existing therapies?

Participants in the study, which had been conducted by the California biotechnology company ChemoCentryx, did appear to have slightly higher rates of symptom remission after one year. But according to the FDA, it was hard to interpret the results because the study’s design had been less than optimal. Some advisory committee members expressed a similar view. The effect of the drug, said one, “is just not well verified by this study.”

At the same time, many patients, and some physicians, saw in the data a potentially helpful medication, one that might lessen patients’ reliance on steroids, which are highly effective but often cause severe side effects.

“Blue dot fever”: Lily Allen dresses up as Ticketmaster unsold seat map for Halloween

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Lily Allen has poked fun at the ticket sales controversy surrounding her recent US tour by dressing up as a Ticketmaster seating map full of unsold tickets for Halloween.

  • READ MORE: Lily Allen live in London: a powerful retelling of one of pop’s most gut-wrenching albums

The singer shared photos of her outfit on Instagram yesterday (October 30), wearing a white puffer jacket and matching bodysuit covered in blue dots, alongside a black top marked “Stage”.

She also posed with a thermometer, referencing the term “blue dot fever”, which has been used throughout 2026 to describe artists struggling to sell tickets for their live shows.

The phrase refers to the blue dots on Ticketmaster’s interactive seating plans, which represent tickets that remain available for purchase. A proliferation of blue dots has become shorthand for disappointing ticket sales and overly ambitious venue bookings.

Allen captioned the photos: “Never beating the allegations. #bluedotfever”.

The joke follows the singer’s decision to downsize several venues on the North American leg of her ‘West End Girl’ tour earlier this year.

In July, she announced that concerts in Chicago, Los Angeles, San Francisco and other cities would be moved to smaller venues, while a planned show in Montreal was cancelled.

Among the changes was the relocation of her Chicago concert from the 23,500-capacity United Center to the 3,500-capacity Chicago Theatre. A planned show at San Francisco’s Chase Center was also moved to Oakland’s smaller Paramount Theatre.

Speaking about the decision in an interview last month, Allen acknowledged: “The ticket sales weren’t there.”

She explained that the original venue bookings had been driven by promoters’ expectations, and that she had expressed concerns about the scale of the tour from the outset.

“I said to my manager, ‘Are you sure? This seems like a stretch.’ And he was like, ‘This is what they’re telling me.’ So we accepted it,” she recalled.

Allen said she had been reluctant to cancel the concerts outright because she had a crew of around 50 people relying on the work.

She also criticised the lack of transparency in concert ticketing, recounting how fans had obtained cheap last-minute tickets for shows she had previously been told were sold out.

“I think the live industry is a minefield, and I don’t know how it works,” she said. “Someone knows, but it ain’t the artist.”

The tour has seen Allen perform her acclaimed 2025 album ‘West End Girl’ in its entirety, in a theatrical production featuring backing tracks rather than a live band, with minimal audience interaction.

She previously defended the unconventional format after facing criticism, explaining: “It’s my artistic choice not to talk to the audience, the fourth wall helps with the storytelling.”

“Everyone on this tour is really working very hard to give people the best show we possibly can, and I’m extremely proud of it.”

NME reviewed the tour at the London Palladium in March, awarding it three stars and describing the production as “a powerful retelling of one of pop’s most gut-wrenching albums”.

The album itself, inspired by the breakdown of Allen’s marriage to Stranger Things actor David Harbour, received a four-star review from NME, which hailed it as “a vicious, vulnerable and victorious comeback”.

Review: The new Kindle Colorsoft is... fine.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The best Kindle e-readers are essential for book lovers, and Amazon just released a new Kindle Colorsoft (and the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition with an aluminum body), along with a new basic Kindle, Kindle Paperwhite/Paperwhite Signature Edition, and the Kindle Click page-turner. Following last year's refresh of the Kindle Scribe lineup, that means Amazon has an all-new Kindle family, just in time for the holidays.

I've been reviewing the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition for Mashable for the past few weeks. Except for the new aluminum body, fig colorway, and higher storage, it's functionally the same as the base Colorsoft model, so we won't be reviewing them separately.

If you've been curious about Amazon's color e-readers, then I'd recommend reading my Kindle Colorsoft review first. Unfortunately, while there's a lot that I do love about this device, it doesn't fix the biggest problem — the color is still very disappointing.

That cover is definitely yellow-ish. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

Kindle Colorsoft review: Manage your expectations when it comes to color

Now, I want to be clear about one thing: Amazon uses E Ink technology for its e-readers. This distinctive, paper-like digital display is a product of the E Ink Corporation. And the truth is that E Ink just doesn't do color very well. So, the technology is what it is.

The E Ink color experience on the Kindle Colorsoft is disappointing. That was true with the original model from 2024, and it hasn't changed with the 2026 model. What has changed is that the Colorsoft now offers true edge-to-edge color, so there are no dimming or lighting issues around the edges.

But Amazon promises readers something more: "Paper-like color perfect for comics, graphic novels, and more." And that's not what I'm seeing. In fact, some of Amazon's official photography with the Kindle Colorsoft appears to exaggerate the color saturation, at least to my eye. I've never seen color quite that bright in practice.

I haven't seen colors quite this bright on my review unit, sadly. Credit: Amazon

I put the Kindle side-by-side with illustrated books, comic books, and graphic novels I have on my bookshelf, and the difference is very obvious, even when you crank up the vividness and brightness to the limit. There's just no comparison, really.

Even in comics with more neutral tones and softer colors like the classic Batman comic The Long Halloween, the difference stands out clearly. How important is the color problem? Well, the word "color" is right there in the name — Kindle Colorsoft. Though, I suppose the emphasis should be on "soft."

The color difference is obvious. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable What elder millennial nerd doesn't own a copy of 'Watchmen'? Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

I have seen more vibrant and colorful electronic paper technology. Samsung's ePaper does a better job with color, though there are other trade-offs. It takes time for the screen to change to a new image, which isn't an option in an e-reader that demands near-instant page turns.

In my photos of Samsung's ePaper, you'll notice brighter colors, but there's a reason Samsung reserves this tech for commercial use.

Samsung's ePaper commercial display technology on display. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable A closer look at Samsung's ePaper commercial display technology. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

If you want to read comics, magazines, or illustrated books in digital form, a tablet like the new iPad Mini or Amazon Alexa tablets would be a better option. (Mashable is in the process of reviewing both of those devices.)

And you can always go to an actual bookstore.

Amazon Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition: What I liked

To be clear, there is a lot to love about the Kindle Colorsoft, particularly the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition. I love the fig color, which is a close match for the new burgundy iPhone 18 Pro. (Fun fact: An Amazon spokesperson told Mashable the fig color was inspired by the same industry trend report that inspired the burgundy iPhone.)

The new aluminum body available in the Paperwhite SE and Colorsoft SE is worth the upgrade. They have a similar feel to the Apple iPad Air, which I also loved. It's also satisfying to see the cover screen artwork and your book covers in color.

Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

I also appreciated the ability to zoom in on comic panels and illustrations, which is a great way to appreciate fine details in the artwork.

Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable ...enhance! Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

You can double-click to read comics panel-by-panel on the Colorsoft, rather than page-by-page. Often, this works really well, but for artists who use complicated panels of varying sizes and styles (like you'll find in The Long Halloween), some of the panels fit very awkwardly on the screen.

The new Kindle Colorsoft also has a lot of the features we loved in other Kindle devices — a waterproof design, a battery that lasts for weeks, auto-adjusting light. Amazon also moved the power button to the side, which is a smart update. Only the Signature Edition supports wireless charging, and it now has magnetic pogo pins on the back. That means if you're used to using Qi wireless charging accessories with your Kindle, they won't work here. For me, this isn't a problem.

To sum up — the Kindle Colorsoft isn't a bad color e-reader. It just suffers from the same problems all color e-readers do.

The Kindle Colorsoft: Who it's for

If you believe some color is better than no color, or if you're a note-taker who loves to highlight their books, then the Kindle Colorsoft would be a smart upgrade from the basic Kindle. (Pro tip: We named the Kindle Scribe Colorsoft a Mashable Choice product, and the ability to write on the screen lets you use color in much better ways.) However, the Kobo Libra Colour is retaining its top spot as our favorite color e-reader.

The Kindle Colorsoft would also be a smart option for kids in the process of graduating from illustrated kids' books to chapter books. The Colorsoft could help make this transition a bit more inviting, and parents can also get a Kindle Colorsoft Kids Edition for $299.99.

When picking an e-reader, price is always a consideration. However, almost all e-readers fall into the same price range. Kindle and Kobo e-readers both start around $149.99/$159.99, while the tiny Boox Picco is just $109.99. If you want handwriting capabilities, Kobo and Boox make the most affordable options, as Kindle's Scribes start at $500.

  • Remarkable PaperPro: Whenever you search for e-readers, Remarkable's tablets will always pop up in the search results, but unfortunately, it's hard to obtain ePUB files on the Remarkable. Where's the Nook on this list?

In terms of big-name e-readers, you might notice one omission from this list: the Barnes & Noble Nook. I have tested this e-reader, and it was previously featured on this list. However, after testing more devices, it no longer makes the cut. In comparison to any of the Kindles or the Kobo Libra, the Nook has sloth-like performance, making it frustrating to use.

Plus, getting my library books onto the Nook was a nightmare, as it was a multi-step process that required downloading to a computer before uploading to a Nook. Its only redeeming quality was the buttons on either side of the device, which made page turning a breeze. However, unless you are a loyal buyer of Barnes & Noble books, I recommend steering clear of the Nook.

Where to get e-reader books for free

Once you get an e-reader, it's time to fill it up with books, and that doesn't necessarily mean spending money. In fact, you can load up your e-reader without spending a dime; you just need to be a bit resourceful.

The best way to read nearly any book — a classic or hot new release — is with Libby. I have read hundreds of free books with the Libby app — I wouldn't have been able to test for this guide without it. It connects with your library card, allowing you to borrow from your local library's digital collection. Plus, both Kobo and Kindle have exceptional Libby integrations.

Another way to build your library is through Stuff Your Kindle Day events. These are 24-hour opportunities led by authors that make books discounted or free.

If you're a fan of the classics or looking to discover a treasure lost to time, an online resource like Project Gutenberg has more than 75,000 books in the public domain that you can download and read on an e-reader.
