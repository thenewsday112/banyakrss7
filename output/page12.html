Modern Warfare 4’s DMZ mode gets 3-day early access on Oct. 20

📅 Oct 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A Call of Duty blog dropped a treasure trove of information for Modern Warfare 4‘s DMZ mode today, including good news for the more eager players. The mode is getting an early-access window starting on Oct. 20, exclusive to those who acquired the game ahead of time.

Modern Warfare 4‘s full release is slated for Friday, Oct. 23, but DMZ is opening its gates three days early if you preordered. The campaign is also available early, on Oct. 16, if you own any edition of the game.

Image via Activision

Read what’s behind the latest news in gaming (with a bit of snark) by subscribing to the Destructoid Checkpoint newsletter!

DMZ is evolving drastically in scope from its old beta into “Call of Duty’s definitive extraction shooter experience,” by the developer’s admission. This pushes it fully into the burgeoning genre, with its own base-building, progression, and even a bounty system for players.

The blog post dove into the Forward Operating Base (FOB), your respite between incursions in Hajin. Here, you’ll manage operators, craft or improve weapons, find vendors, and do general housekeeping when you’re not in a sortie.

It also showcased the deep operator progression, including operator levels, prestige bonuses, and three skill trees. The game has an MIA system for recovering lost agents using DMZ cash, which reminds me of Rainbow Six Extraction (and I might be the only one who remembers it).

Opening up early access to a higher-stakes, punishing extraction-based game mode is bound to draw criticism from parts of the fanbase, however. The three days of extra progression could provide an advantage over players dropping in on release, and that asymmetry would be more troublesome in the extraction mode than in multiplayer or Warzone.

As someone who played the old DMZ with my friends almost every night, there seems to be enough depth to give it some longevity, especially if it gets regular updates and tweaks every few months. With Battlefield 6‘s season five launching on Oct. 13, I think I might have to ask my boss for time off for “an early Halloween.”

Ubisoft is shutting down this forgotten Rainbow Six game you probably didn’t even know existed

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Rainbow Six franchise has a wealth of installments, and when it comes to this amount of volume, you can’t always get them right. That’s the case for Rainbow Six Mobile, which is shutting down in January 2027, Ubisoft announced today.

The game will stay up until Jan. 15, 2027, the developer said, and its upcoming season “will be released as planned next week.” The announcement teases more updates to prepare for the transition, though it’s unclear what this means in practice.

Rainbow Six Mobile has had an unusual run since its reveal in 2022. At the time, Ubisoft said it was a version of Rainbow Six Siege built from the ground up to “adapt the Siege experience to mobile devices.”

Rainbow Six Extraction, the other forgotten R6 spinoff. Image via Ubisoft

Despite a few closed tests and soft launches after 2023 and a litany of changes in 2024, the game only released globally in February 2026. It has had less than eight months in the hands of players all over the world before being sunset—which, as we all know by now, is the “your dog went to live on a farm” of the gaming industry.

At first glance, Rainbow Six Mobile may almost seem doomed to fail. Siege‘s identity is built on its tactical, unforgiving gameplay—the kind that may get you to hold an angle with an ACOG on your gun, so tensely you can barely breathe. It’s a hard element to bring to a mobile game.

“Our goal is not to have you stop playing on PC or console and switch to mobile,” the team said on the announcement blog post. “We know there is nothing like the adrenaline rush of a close Siege match on the big screen or the precision of a mouse and keyboard.” Instead, the game aimed to let fans “get the R6 experience” in “short, accessible bursts,” the developers said.

It’s unclear what will happen to Rainbow Six Mobile until its shutdown date, but Ubisoft will announce more of its plans “in the coming weeks,” according to its announcement.

Spider-Man and One Piece are getting an official crossover…kind of

📅 Oct 01, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The Amazing Spider-Man has officially reached 1,000 numbered issues, and to celebrate, Marvel’s best artists are whipping up awe-inspiring variant covers for the seminal title. And thanks to one of them, New York’s resident Web-Swinger has, in a roundabout way, stepped into the wild world of One Piece.

Okay, so it’s not an official crossover. Like, at all. But it’s still an amazing example of one media juggernaut inspiring another. And it’s far from the first time Marvel or American comics have paid homage to anime.

The “crossover” cover, drawn by veteran Marvel artist, depicts an unmasked Spider-Man striking a fist-to-ground stance as billowing steam rolls off his body. To the uninitiated, this might look like a fairly generic superhero pose. But to One Piece fans (and anyone even a little familiar with anime and manga), the pose is an obvious reference.

View this post on Instagram

Like all the best anime protagonists, One Piece‘s Monkey D. Luffy has a few transformations, or “Gears,” up his non-existent sleeves, and each one amps up his innate rubber-based powers a few notches. While Luffy has reached Gear 5 as of the most recent manga chapters, his Gear 2 form might be the fan favorite, largely thanks to the pose he strikes when he first unlocks it.

The moment when Luffy first unleashes Gear 2 is one of the most memorable in all of One Piece. Facing off against a world-class assassin to rescue a captured friend from the totalitarian government that rules his world, Luffy defies both the law and the limits of his own body, reaching a new tier of power while striking an objectively cool pose.

This pose, widely known as the “Gear 2” pose, is a stance nearly every manga and anime can spot from miles away, and it’s made its way into other forms of media, including DC’s Absolute Batman.

View this post on Instagram

Honestly, the Gear 2 pose is a perfect fit for a hero like Spider-Man. If there’s one thing everyone knows about Peter Parker, it’s that the guy is just too damn cool. No matter how hard the world (aka Marvel’s ever-shifting team of writers) knocks him down, Spider-Man always gets back up, usually with a cold quip and an even colder counter-blow.

While we’ll probably never see an official Spider-Man x One Piece crossover, this variant cover is a fun look at what can happen when one form of media is allowed to reference another without the Sword of Damocles of copyright hanging over their head.

CONTROL Resonant is pretty stellar on its own, but the game will get even better in the next couple of months. Remedy is adding the coveted Photo Mode in November, plus adding a “New Game ++” next month, the studio announced today.

CONTROL Resonant has some of the most breathtaking environments in Remedy history, with altered Manhattan and the Unknown Zone being particularly scenic. Though the game released recently, fans are already vehemently asking for a Photo Mode, and the developer listened.

“The Photo Mode for CONTROL Resonant is something we’ve been cooking for a while,” Remedy said in a blog post today. “We even consulted some talented virtual photographers from the community to help us make it the best it can be.”

Some tricks are good, but not fail-proof. Screenshot by Destructoid

Despite being a beloved feature from the first CONTROL, Photo Mode wasn’t available in Resonant at launch “because we needed to prioritize making sure the game was in the best possible shape,” the post adds, but the feature is scheduled for “sometime in November.”

In the meantime, players can get a faux Photo Mode by getting Dylan to face a wall and rotating the camera around him. Done from the right angle, he disappears from the frame, but this is an imperfect solution that doesn’t work everywhere.

Though highly anticipated, the addition of Photo Mode isn’t entirely surprising for the community. CONTROL also added the feature after launch, and given how much work Remedy poured into the environments in the sequel, allowing fans to screenshot to their heart’s content is a natural choice.

Before Photo Mode hits, Remedy is also making a big improvement to the New Game Plus experience by letting you replay it as often as you like. As it stands, you only get the first playthrough and an encore, after which you’re stuck with the large (but finite) amount of side quests and secrets.

Screenshot by Destructoid

With the new addition, however, you can run as many subsequent playthroughs as you want, offering you more ways to progress through the game’s multiple zones. You also get the chance to revisit the main story and side quests, opting for different dialogue if you missed it the first time around. Those parallel stories often have multiple endings, so you may want to replay them more than once or twice. “New Game ++” is scheduled to launch in October.

CONTROL Resonant shot up to the top of Steam’s best-selling list when it released yesterday, marking another successful entry into the Remedyverse. We gave it a valiant nine out of 10 for its fluid combat, stunning environments, and delightful exploration, which are bound to become even better after the studio adds these two features.

Art Garfunkel has admitted that he is considering “leaving America” due to global warming and Donald Trump.

Speaking in a new interview with The Guardian, Garfunkel expressed his dismay over the current administration in the United States under President Trump.

“I don’t like America’s position at all. I’m not aboard, and I’m thinking of leaving the country, frankly. It’s real that the planet is getting warmer, that the water’s rising, that so many people are starving,” he said.

“When Donald Trump took over Venezuela, are those the new rules? Just take over a country and run it? It’s shocking, and you think: how on earth are we gonna change the presidency?”

His comments come eight months after US forces captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro in Caracas and brought him to the United States to face federal charges.

Despite Garfunkel’s pessimism he added: “Trump won’t last forever. So we’ve got to hope.”

Garfunkel is not the only celebrity who has threatened to quit the US because of Trump.

Previously, Avatar director James Cameron said he was “sickened” by Trump’s re-election in 2024 and admitted the he was happy his New Zealand citizenship was “imminent”.

“I see a turn away from everything decent,” he said at the time. “America doesn’t stand for anything if it doesn’t stand for what it has historically stood for. It becomes a hollow idea, and I think they’re hollowing it out as fast as they can for their own benefit.”

Comedian and TV host Rosie O’Donnell also moved to Ireland when Trump was re-elected.

“I miss many things about life there at home and I’m trying to find a home here in this beautiful country and when it is safe for all citizens to have equal rights there in America, that’s when we will consider coming back,” she said at the time. “It’s been heartbreaking to see what’s happening politically and hard for me personally as well. The personal is political, as we all know”.

Her comments prompted Trump to threaten to remove her US citizenship.

Meanwhile, Art Garfunkel recently opened up to NME about ending “years of detachment” from Paul Simon.

Simon & Garfunkel were one of the best-selling acts of the 1960s, selling over 100 million records and recording timeless hits such as ‘Bridge Over Troubled Water’, ‘The Sound of Silence’ and ‘Mrs. Robinson’.

However, they also became known for their troubled relationship, with artistic disagreements leading to their breakup in 1970. They have performed together sporadically since then, but not since 2010.

The latest feud followed a particularly razor-tongued 2015 interview Garfunkel gave to The Telegraph, where the singer labelled his former musical cohort a “jerk” and an “idiot” – an outburst he said he subsequently regretted.

Speaking about a recent meeting with Simon, he told NME: “Paul said to me, ‘Arty, it’s not that you spoke to the British press and that you didn’t do it well…[it’s that] I felt you wanted to hurt me. That’s what got me’. And I thought, ‘That’s true’. I wanted to hurt him. The next thing I knew, I burst into tears… and then there were hugs.”

While this is a fine enough way to structure an SCP film, I can’t help but feel that the makers would be better off drawing inspiration from arguably the most iconic piece of SCP media, 2012’s SCP-Containment Breach game.

SCP-Containment Breach is a fantastic blueprint for an SCP Foundation movie

Image via Steam

The SCP Foundation is an Internet-born collaborative fiction project centered on the titular paranormal research collective dedicated to studying anomalous and often dangerous entities and artifacts known as SCPs. All kinds of media have spawned from the project, but SCP-Containment Breach is arguably the best known.

An exploration-driven first-person horror title, SCP-Containment Breach is an interactive crash course in the lore. Placed in the shoes of a dispensable D-Class Foundation employee, players must navigate a compromised SCP containment facility, outwitting, outmaneuvering, or flat-out outrunning many of the franchise’s best-known SCPs.

While its star has faded in recent years, SCP-Containment Breach is a quintessential cornerstone in the Foundation’s history, putting the project on countless people’s radars. With that in mind, it’s mind-boggling that the creators of V/H/S SCP aren’t drawing inspiration from it.

It’s no secret that Hollywood is looking to video games for inspiration, and that films inspired by indie horror games have been busting the box office wide open lately. However, the biggest success stories from this new age of video game adaptation have something V/H/S: SCP seems to lack: a strong central story pulled by a relatable main character.

From Iron Lung to Station 8, video game-based horror movies seem to work best when they have a strong lead carrying the plot forward. As fascinating as Kane Parsons’ take on the Backrooms is, the personal struggles of the film’s leads were the beating heart of the film, turning what could have been a quick-and-easy horror story into something more endearing and haunting.

An anthological SCP Foundation movie isn’t a bad idea, and it sounds like the film’s creators are trying to recreate the series’ iconic, analog aesthetic. That said, not framing the film around a central character feels like a missed opportunity. Imagine how much more impactful the horrors the SCP Foundation hides would be if the character they’re menacing is someone the audience cared about.

Without something to bind the film’s shorts together, V/H/S: SCP will have to polish each vignette to near-perfection, milking the unique terror of each featured SCP for all it’s worth. If done well, this could lead to a series of incredibly terrifying shorts. Otherwise, it risks being a blemish on this recent golden wave of indie video game movies.

Iron Lung is out early on YouTube

In a video posted to his YouTube channel, Fischbach confirmed Iron Lung‘s sudden release wasn’t a mistake or a prank. Barely keeping his frustration with the site’s antiquated distribution model in check, he revealed Iron Lung is currently available for purchase or rental on YouTube in the United States, Australia, Canada, Great Britain, and New Zealand. Unfortunately for those who prefer more traditional streaming services, Iron Lung is a YouTube exclusive.
