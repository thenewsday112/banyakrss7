Over 25% of people lie to get antibiotics—and their lies aren't even good

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

A significant number of patients in the US lie to their doctors to get antibiotics they likely don't need, according to two new studies. While that may not be a startling revelation, the studies drills down on the phenomenon, finding that the lies patients tell are generally bad ones—and yet, they work almost all the time.

In the two linked studies published Friday in JAMA Network Open, a team of researchers led by Alistair Thorpe at the University of Utah analyzed online survey data from over 1,000 adults. They found that over a quarter of people reported having lied to a clinician at least once to get an antibiotic prescription for themselves or a child in their care. Patients lied by either exaggerating symptoms, making up symptoms, or—most often—both.

But interestingly, the lies people told to get antibiotics involved making up or exaggerating symptoms of infections that wouldn't be treated with antibiotics. For example, the most common falsehoods were symptoms associated with colds, such as fever, cough, and sore throat. That's despite the fact that colds are caused by viruses, not bacteria—which antibiotics treat.

Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 will remove real-world footage of the 2022 Seoul crush incident after severe backlash in South Korea. Bereaved families issued a statement yesterday condemning the inclusion of these videos, and Activision has since vowed to cut them from the title.

The game is set in the Korean Peninsula, and real-world footage of several events is shown in the opening cutscene as a means to ground its narrative in reality.

Among the videos are scenes of the 2022 Seoul crush incident, which happened during a Halloween celebration on Oct. 29 in Itaewon, when a crowd suddenly surged and began to trample or crush those within it. Around 160 deaths were confirmed, most of whom were young adults, making it one of the deadliest events in South Korean history.

Families of those who perished or were injured in the incident issued a statement yesterday condemning the inclusion of this footage, telling the BBC they were “appalled.”

“We cannot help but be appalled that the chaotic and desperate scene, in which many citizens at the site performed CPR to save one another and victims lay on the freezing street, was used to create a scene in a game,” they said.

“Using such an event as material for a game made for entertainment is in itself highly inappropriate and senseless,” they added.

Modern Warfare 4 is largely set in the Korean Peninsula during a “New Korean War.” Image via Activision

Activision has since responded to the backlash, promising to cut the scenes entirely in the game’s next update on Nov. 12. The company told the BBC it never “intended to reference or trivialize the Itaewon disaster or any other real-world tragedy in any way, shape or form,” but merely used real-world footage to frame the game’s fictional story.

The Modern Warfare franchise, both the original trilogy and these new games, is well known for its shocking and mortifying scenes. However, the difference here is that, while the most horrific moments in the games were all fictional, Infinity Ward used real footage of a tragedy. We can debate whether showing it here was distasteful or dehumanizing or reductive, but I can see where these families are coming from.

If we start drawing lines in the sand too much, though, art will have no way to push forward, and addressing reality will become impossible. Rare meteorite from Mars gives us a view into its ancient crust

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Crater counts tell us that most of the Martian surface is more than 3 billion years old, but pieces of Mars we have on Earth tell a different story. The three main classes of Martian meteorites—shergottites, nakhlites, and chassignites—are all 2.4 billion years old or younger. Until now, only two genuinely ancient Martian meteorites were known: Allan Hills 84001, found in Antarctica, and a jumbled rock from northwest Africa nicknamed Black Beauty. Now we have a third one.

A team led by Yang Liu, a planetary scientist at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, has shown that a Martian meteorite called Teghaza 001, found in the Sahara Desert in Mali in 2022, formed at least 4.1 billion years ago. “It's very old and similar to some of the oldest rocks on Earth,” Liu says.

A filtered collection

“We don't fully understand why old Martian rocks are so rare on Earth," Liu said. Part of the answer may lie in how a piece of Mars gets here. It takes a large impact to launch a rock off the planet, and not every rock can survive that. “If the impact hits into loose powder, it may not get cemented to become a rock and fly off Mars' gravity,” Liu said. “So the natural impact already acts as a filter. Weak rocks do not make it off Mars.”

A tale of 2 exclusives in the multiplatform era: Wolverine helps sell PS5s, but Gears doesn’t move the needle for Xbox

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft’s attempts to win back consumers by making Gears of War: E-Day an exclusive has not panned out so far, according to industry analysis.

New Xbox CEO Asha Sharma’s “reset,” including a return to console exclusivity for Gears, “seems to be getting Twitter likes from the most passionate part of their fan base,” according to Circana’s Mat Piscatella in a recent video for The Game Business, “but other than that, in terms of actual results, I’m not seeing anything right now.”

Image via Xbox

While E-Day is critically acclaimed with an 87 Metacritic rating (I scored it a 9 for Destructoid myself), it likely hasn’t translated to the sales Microsoft was hoping for. According to The Verge, it’s sold over 400,000 copies while being available on Game Pass. And none of that has made a dent when it comes to selling consoles, either.

“Saying Gears is going to be exclusive hasn’t made any difference in terms of the hardware trend,” Piscatella said. “In fact, it keeps getting worse. And Gears is a gorgeous game. That is a Gears-ass Gears of War game. But by making it exclusive, you chop the legs out from under its potential as an individual title in order to boost your platform, and at least in the short term, it hasn’t made a difference.”

So far, Xbox’s “exclusivity as a selling point” after years of its confusing “This is an Xbox” marketing hasn’t done much when it comes to getting Xbox hardware off shelves, which is also understandable given we’re approaching the end of this generation. But on the other side of the aisle, a PS5 exclusive in Marvel’s Wolverine has been paying off for the competitor despite its mixed reviews (75 on Metacritic), according to Piscatella.

“If you look at the weekly sales cadence of accessories and hardware, the Wolverine DualSense was the bestselling accessory of the month,” Piscatella said. “The [console] bundle sold out. There weren’t huge quantities, but that really helped boost PlayStation 5 sales that particular week. In terms of an old-school, ‘here’s an exclusive game and we’re going to sell some consoles and some accessories with it,’ I think it’s about as good as you could hope from an IP that isn’t the level of Spider-Man.”

Image via PlayStation

There’s no doubt that the upcoming Grand Theft Auto 6 release is also helping to sell consoles for PlayStation, which is being marketed as the “best” place to play the highly anticipated sequel. The game is also available on Xbox, though, and hardware sales fell 14 percent year-over-year last quarter, according to Microsoft itself, but Sharma said it has to do with not being able to meet demand.

I’m bored of console wars in general and wish that all games were multiplatform. But if you’re using exclusivity as a selling point and it’s not working, at what point do you pivot again?

The film was made in collaboration with visual artist Lauren Tsai and arrives as the band continue celebrating the 20th anniversary of their landmark 2006 album ‘The Black Parade’.

Produced by Tsai’s independent studio UNREAL INC., the project combines the visual world of her animated film The Dying World with ‘The End.’, the opening track from the album.

The nine-minute film follows Astrid, a student at a mysterious New England boarding school that has become overrun with rubbish.

When the school’s authorities throw away her cherished sketchbook, she follows it down a rubbish chute and finds herself transported into a surreal world inhabited by forgotten ideas.

Tsai voices Astrid herself, while Eilish plays her friend and Bridgers voices her sister. Japanese video game designer Hideo Kojima plays a character known as The Teacher, while Scrubs actor John C. McGinley voices The Dean.

The project marks a full-circle moment for Tsai, who grew up creating animated fan videos set to My Chemical Romance’s music.

“It was an absolute dream to work on this project for The Black Parade and The Dying World,” Tsai said in a statement.

She added that she deeply respected frontman Gerard Way‘s creative legacy, and that she was honoured he had drawn inspiration from her own work, having spent years admiring the worlds he created.

Tsai previously worked with Bridgers in 2023, when she directed the animated music video for Boygenius’ ‘Cool About It’.

Speaking to NME about that project, she said: “‘Cool About It’ is a beautifully melancholic and piercing song that puts words to feelings often unexplained. It is an absolute dream to collaborate with the genius that is Boygenius.”

Bridgers is also a longtime admirer of My Chemical Romance, having covered ‘Welcome To The Black Parade’ alongside Sloppy Jane at SXSW in 2022.

Meanwhile, Eilish and Bridgers have previously performed together, with the latter joining her alongside Boygenius bandmates Lucy Dacus and Julien Baker for a rendition of ‘When The Party’s Over’ at London’s Electric Ballroom in 2023.

‘Prelude To The End’ will also be screened ahead of My Chemical Romance’s final two Hollywood Bowl concerts tonight (October 30) and tomorrow (October 31).

The shows will conclude the US leg of their ‘Long Live The Black Parade’ anniversary tour, which has seen the band perform the 2006 record in full alongside selections from the rest of their catalogue.

The performances have made headlines after the company behind the Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders launched legal action against the band over the use of lookalike uniforms during a staged gunfight.

Meanwhile, MCR released an expanded 20th anniversary edition of ‘The Black Parade’ last Friday (October 23), featuring six bonus tracks, including previously unreleased recordings from a 2007 show in Hoboken, New Jersey.

In its original 9/10 review, NME praised the record’s theatrical ambition, writing that it belonged alongside ‘American Idiot’, ‘Doolittle’ and ‘Nevermind’ among the greatest American rock albums.

Elsewhere, Way recently told fans that the group hope to record another album, although no plans have been confirmed.

“We’ve kind of really always wanted to make another record, so we hope that life allows us to do that,” he said. “You guys deserve another record. I don’t know when it’ll be. But it’ll be awesome.”
