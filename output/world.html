Deep Purple postpone shows after Ian Gillan hospitalised following “medical emergency”

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Deep Purple have been forced to postpone two concerts in Germany after frontman Ian Gillan was hospitalised following a “medical emergency”.

  • READ MORE: Deep Purple – ‘Whoosh!’ review: rockers’ 21st record is stupidly fun and outrageously silly

The legendary British rock band were due to perform at the QI Arena in Leipzig last night (October 31) and the Sporthalle in Hamburg tonight (November 1), as part of their ongoing ‘Splat!’ world tour.

However, the group announced on Saturday that both performances would be postponed after their 81-year-old singer was taken to hospital.

“With great disappointment, Deep Purple must announce that they are unable to perform as scheduled tonight at the QI Arena (Leipzig) and tomorrow evening at the Sporthalle (Hamburg),” they wrote in a statement.

“This comes as a result of a medical emergency involving Ian Gillan, which has resulted in his current hospitalisation.”

The band said they understood the disappointment caused by the short-notice announcement and confirmed that they were working to arrange replacement dates for both concerts.

They added that all existing tickets would remain valid for the rescheduled performances, signing off their message: “With the greatest love and respect… Deep Purple x”.

View this post on Instagram

No further details have been shared about the nature of Gillan’s medical emergency or his condition.

The band are currently scheduled to resume their European tour at the Westfalenhalle in Dortmund on Tuesday (November 3), followed by a concert at Berlin’s Uber Arena the following evening.

The tour will subsequently reach the UK, with concerts scheduled for Newcastle (November 18), Glasgow (19), Birmingham (21), Manchester (22) and London (24 and 25).

The final UK date will see the band return to London’s Royal Albert Hall, the venue where they premiered their orchestral composition ‘Concerto For Group And Orchestra’ in 1969.

The tour comes in support of their 24th studio album ‘Splat!’, which was released in July via earMUSIC and was produced by longtime collaborator Bob Ezrin.

Last November, Gillan revealed that he was losing his eyesight and suggested that retirement was “not far off”, explaining that he had only around 30 per cent of his vision remaining, although he said he had adapted to the challenges it presented.

Asked about the possibility of retiring, Gillan said: “I think if I lose my energy I’m going to stop. I don’t want to be an embarrassment to anyone.”

Earlier this year, the band also made headlines when founding guitarist Ritchie Blackmore reunited with his former bandmates for the first time in 33 years.

Blackmore joined Gillan, Roger Glover and Ian Paice onstage in New York in August for a performance of ‘Smoke On The Water’, later describing the reunion as “very emotional”.

How Pures 24-hour chat timer forces daters to skip the endless banter

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

One of the major annoyances of online dating is how often matches devolve into pen pals rather than translate into IRL dates. Those initial, flirty conversations are great, don't get us wrong, but most of us aren't jumping onto Tinder or Hinge just to endlessly text. And yet, in modern dating, where both people often feel afraid to make the first move and take the match into the real world, endless texting is all too often the only result of a once-promising match. 

But it doesn't have to be this way. Dating app Pure concocted some clever solutions to the never-ending chatting, and in our own experimentation, they work like a charm. 

Here's how Pure introduced a sense of urgency into its dating mechanics, and how that helps users translate matches into real-life encounters. 

Opens in a new window Credit: Pure Pure   Learn More

How Pure leverages a sense of urgency to benefit its users

One unique feature of Pure is that it doesn't use traditional dating profiles, which means its matches operate differently than on basically every other dating app. 

Instead of having a permanent, public-facing profile, Pure users can post "ads," temporary personal advertisements shouting out who they are or what they want, whether that's for a long-term relationship or a short-term connection. It's only when someone has posted an ad that they're discoverable to other users, and only then are your basic profile elements (age, gender, sexual orientation, and photos) visible. Once your ad expires, so does access to your profile.

The result of this mechanic is a sense of urgency, the very real possibility that you're seeing someone you may never see again, so if you want a connection, you have to act fast. That's a very welcome change from the dating app norm, where profiles are always visible, and you can browse them at your convenience, whether on your morning commute or between sets at the gym.

But Pure takes things a step further with how you actually interact with other people. 

The disappearing chat mechanic

From the moment you initiate a chat on Pure, you're under an artificial time constraint, since every chat automatically self-deletes after 24 hours, unless both parties agree to suspend the chat timer. 

While at first this seems like an unnecessary constraint, in practice it's actually very healthy and helps daters avoid the endless banter that plagues so many online dating interactions. 

Think about what would happen if you approached a cute stranger in the real world. You may see them at a bar or coffee shop, going about their day, but no matter how smooth your introduction, you know in the back of your mind that you can't monopolize this person's time and attention for very long. 

For an in-person approach, you have to be concise in your communication: I think you're cute and want a chance to get to know you better — would that interest you? But on an app, where there's no sense of urgency, you can endlessly waffle, delaying the most important question of all in favor of a never-ending series of mundane questions. 

This is why Pure's disappearing chat mechanic is so clever, even when both parties agree to disable it, since even that act forces a mutual acknowledgment of the connection. And after using the app over an extended period, we can confirm that Pure's culture really encourages in-person interactions at the expense of endless chatting or swiping.

This month, I had the amazing opportunity to join all of you in the WoW: Forever beta and see all the new and exciting features Blizzard introduced into the Vanilla experience to make it fresh and intriguing for everyone, not just the old heads who were around in 2004. I absolutely loved what I saw, even though some archaic Vanilla mechanics remain in place.

The Skyborne race was my faction of choice, aligned with the Alliance of course. I was a Rogue, like most of my characters in WoW, except in post-WOTLK expansions where I would pick anything, level it to 55, and then go and make a blood Death Knight.

I made a Rogue Skyborne as my character of choice. Image via Blizzard

This new race’s starting zone was refreshing. It had a plethora of different areas clearly drawing inspiration from other locations in Vanilla WoW, and moving from one place to another gave you a sense of progression through its environmental storytelling alone. It boasted a lot of cool ancient temples, alongside dark forests shrouded in mist and mystery and strange cities attuned to the very world and the powers of the wind.

The leveling in general was fine. The quests mostly followed the usual MMORPG formula of go there, kill that many creatures, and by chance loot them to bring me their unique items. That’s the overall gist, but some of the quests did have narrative grounding, and the writing in the quests themselves was actually better than I had expected of modern WoW. However, the structure of the zone meant a lot of players skipped most of its content and never saw the ultimate showdown, i.e., the zone’s narrative conclusion.

This is both good and bad. The game does not force you to stay in this zone for much longer than you want to, and as soon as you’re fed up and done, you can go to one of the ships and reach Dalaran, Stormwind, or any other faction capital you want to visit. Once there, you get quests that take you across the world and allow you to pick up leveling from areas in regular Vanilla WoW, but staying in the starting Skyborne zone is an option.

Europa Universalis 5’s Across the Pillars DLC adds nice Iberian flavors but lacks a little spice

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Europa Universalis 5‘s new DLC, Across the Pillars, focuses on adding flavor to the Iberian Peninsula, specifically Castille (Spain), Granada, and Morocco. It tremendously increases the value of playing in this wonderful part of Europe but ultimately falls short of giving you enough unique events and decisions that really turn the gameplay around.

I spent most of my time playing as Castille, looking to proclaim the Spanish Monarchy after uniting the crown of Aragon with my own, following the historical path that led to the eventual rise (and fall) of the Spanish Empire. The DLC opens the game up with a unique situation, the Battle for the Strait, in which Morocco and Castille vie for control over the last remnants of Al-Andalus—the Emirate of Granada. Castille, aka myself, wants to conquer and integrate it into the later Spain, while Morocco has its own agenda and seeks to either expand itself or reclaim the glory of the Umayyads in Iberia.

The new situation is kind of dry, but still good to have. Image via Paradox Interactive

It took several tries, but I eventually conquered Granada fully. It declared an independence war, which I had trouble containing long enough for Morocco to sweep in, proclaim Granada its subject, and take over as the war leader. The independence war no longer being the main CB, I was allowed to conquer Granada’s territories. It was rather easy since I had total continental dominance compared to Morocco, even if they held the upper hand on the sea at the time.

But to my surprise the situation just… ended? The events leading up to it were cool and flavored, but the situation itself simply had very few decisions you could make as Castille. For example, I could only dismantle Granada’s forts, reduce its sovereignty, and demand Muslim scholars from it and convert them to Christianity. That was about it, though I could also pressure its court to gain greater control over the country. Furthermore, Granada simply wants to be independent from you, and nothing really helped. Even at +150 relations, Granada was severely disloyal and declared numerous independence wars against me.

The new expeditions were an actual blast, though. Image via Paradox Interactive

Morocco and Tlemcen also were super warlike and kept declaring wars, disrupting my gameplay, for no particular reason. They couldn’t take any territories, seldom had naval supremacy, and never, and I mean never, had any upper hand on land. The Battle for the Strait thus devolves into a waiting game whereby you actually want to give Granada over to Morocco and then conquer it willy-nilly, since Granada’s countless independence wars do not allow you to do basically anything against it in a peace deal, forcing white peace.

A much stronger side of the DLC are the new expeditions, which all tie into your historical status as a colonial nation searching far and wide for mythical cities and locations. These expeditions are a much better way of exploring than simply sending out exploration missions as they come with a ton of flavor and unique events and can lead to some incredible finds and rapid expansion into the New World. As someone who absolutely adores colonial gameplay in every EU game, I found this to be the strongest and most interesting part of the DLC.

The Iberian Union did not work out well for me since for some reason Aragon kept taking it over despite my ruler being the only son of the king and queen who formed it. I eventually gained control somehow and started integrating the Catalonians into the ultimate Spanish state. A few of my runs fell through because of France, whose levy-spam strategy still works in the game, and because I am completely dumb in economics.

Overall, though, I think this DLC definitely improves the state of affairs in Iberia. It’s better than not having it, even if the main situation is a bit dry. The DLC spans hundreds of in-game years, through many different eras and ages, and it will keep you engaged and entertained for a very long time indeed.

Death Stranding anime’s first trailer is so epic it might finally help people grasp it isn’t a walking simulator at all

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The first trailer for the Death Stranding anime is now out. It’s brief, punchy, and sets the stage for the entire show in under 60 seconds. We get to see Beached Things’ iconic footprints, a character struggling to understand her own circumstances and why she can sense such entities, and, of course, a massive crater. There are always craters.

The trailer came out last night, confirming a 2027 release on Disney+. Death Stranding: Isolations is the full title, and is being produced by Kojima’s studio in collaboration with E&H, a Japanese animation company founded by former MAPPA director Sunghoo Park. It previously worked on the ONA Bullet/Bullet, as well as Ninja Kanui and Red Cat Ramen.

First trailer for the ‘DEATH STRANDING’ 2D anime series.

— DiscussingFilm (@DiscussingFilm) October 30, 2026

The trailer opens up with a courier standing on the outer rim of a crater caused by a Beached Thing (BT) explosion, which, if you are not aware, happens when a BT entity swallows a living creature. The event is known as a voidout and is equivalent to several thermonuclear bombs going off at once. It’s a core part of the Death Stranding universe and the story of Sam Porter, the protagonist, who seems to be present in this initial scene. It could be any other courier wearing the Bridges uniform, however.

Later on, the trailer shifts to the perspective of a young girl who seems to be using a VR headset to help her sleep, and is then approached by a strange entity. Whether this is a BT, as she seems to be dreaming of them, is unclear. She wakes up, startled, and that’s where the trailer ends. It’s all rather ominous and mysterious, but that’s par for the course.

Death Stranding is one of my favorite games of all time, and that includes its direct sequel, On the Beach. Both are set in our own world where the veil between the living and the dead is completely broken and passage is freely allowed between them. Of course, some things were never meant to mix, and just like Dan Brown’s antimatter in Angels and Demons, so do the dead cause catastrophic damage to those still drawing breath.

The story is emotional, packed with fantastic characters and actors, and writing that, even if a bit over-the-top at times, is seldom outmatched by any other game currently available. It’s definitely a masterclass in game design, but unfortunately for its sales and status, a lot of people have been sharing fake information about it, calling it a walking simulator and being very reductive about what the game actually is.

And I hope this anime, whenever it does air, will bring a bigger audience into this amazing world and inspire them to play the game and see that all they’ve been hearing is, well, complete bulls**t.

The Amazing Spider-Man has officially reached 1,000 numbered issues, and to celebrate, Marvel’s best artists are whipping up awe-inspiring variant covers for the seminal title. And thanks to one of them, New York’s resident Web-Swinger has, in a roundabout way, stepped into the wild world of One Piece.

Okay, so it’s not an official crossover. Like, at all. But it’s still an amazing example of one media juggernaut inspiring another. And it’s far from the first time Marvel or American comics have paid homage to anime.

The “crossover” cover, drawn by veteran Marvel artist, depicts an unmasked Spider-Man striking a fist-to-ground stance as billowing steam rolls off his body. To the uninitiated, this might look like a fairly generic superhero pose. But to One Piece fans (and anyone even a little familiar with anime and manga), the pose is an obvious reference.

Like all the best anime protagonists, One Piece‘s Monkey D. Luffy has a few transformations, or “Gears,” up his non-existent sleeves, and each one amps up his innate rubber-based powers a few notches. While Luffy has reached Gear 5 as of the most recent manga chapters, his Gear 2 form might be the fan favorite, largely thanks to the pose he strikes when he first unlocks it.

The moment when Luffy first unleashes Gear 2 is one of the most memorable in all of One Piece. Against the Storm’s upcoming 1.4 patch brings delicious fish and glue

📅 Aug 06, 2024  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Hooded Horse and Eremite Games have provided a preview of Against the Storm’s upcoming 1.4 patch. This major update will bring major content additions to the base game. However, its release date has not yet been announced.

The major focus of the 1.4 patch is to bring in additional resources and production. The marquee feature here is fishing, which is done at ponds. These are resource nodes, much like many of the others that already exist within the game world. These are exploited using fishing huts.

There are also new buildings, resources, needs, and orders. With the addition of new resources and blueprints, Eremite Games acknowledges that this could result in bloat. The random (near-roguelite) nature of Against the Storm requires that you make effective use of what’s available to you rather than allowing you to rely on one single strategy. However, if too much gets added into there, it can dilute available strategies, making some sessions more difficult than others.

Image via Eremite Games

In order to address this, they’re adding in new systems for unlocking things, such as relying on reputation from villager species as requirements. When I reviewed Against the Storm (which I’ll remind you was one of my favorite games of the year) at its full launch, one of the things I praised most was its ability to juggle so many systems and facets without it weakening the overall experience. I can absolutely see how adding more into it could make it unbalanced, but, at this point, Eremite Games has my faith that they can do it.

Eremite also announced that, in late August, they’ll reveal the new species and new biome that will be added in the upcoming paid DLC. They also announced that they will announce the release date of patch 1.4 at that time. They also remarked that this is an announcement of an announcement. Having worked in this role for a while now, I recognize that announcing an announcement is pretty standard, but it’s still rather weird.

Against the Storm is available now for PC. The 1.4 update and paid DLC does not currently have a set release date.
