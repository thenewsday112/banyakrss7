Meet the real-life Scottish surgeon who inspired Sherlock Holmes

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Sir Arthur Conan Doyle created one of the most beloved fictional detectives of all time in Sherlock Holmes, spawning countless novels, films, TV series, and so forth featuring the sleuth. Far fewer people know about the real-life inspiration for Holmes: a renowned surgeon at Scotland's University of Edinburgh named Joseph Bell, who taught Doyle when the author was still a young medical student. Bell is the subject of a new, eminently readable biography, Sleuth-Hound: The Case of the Real Sherlock Holmes, by medical historian Lindsey Fitzharris.

A noted science communicator with a fondness for the medically macabre, Fitzharris published a biography of Scottish surgical pioneer Joseph Lister, The Butchering Art, in 2017—a great, if occasionally grisly, read. She followed up with 2022’s  The Facemaker: A Visionary Surgeon’s Battle to Mend the Disfigured Soldiers of World War I, about a WWI surgeon named Harold Gillies who rebuilt the faces of injured soldiers.

In 2020, Fitzharris hosted a documentary for the Smithsonian Channel, The Curious Life and Death Of…, exploring famous deaths, ranging from drug lord Pablo Escobar to magician Harry Houdini. Fitzharris performed virtual autopsies, experimented with blood samples, interviewed witnesses, and conducted real-time demonstrations in hopes of gleaning fresh insights. And last year she and her husband, caricaturist Adrian Teal, collaborated on an illustrated children's book, Dead Ends! Flukes, Flops, and Failures that Sparked Medical Marvels.

Moderna and Merck say their mRNA cancer vaccine cut death, recurrence by 42%

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Personalized mRNA vaccines against melanoma—the deadliest form of skin cancer—reduced the risk of death or recurrence by 42 percent compared to standard treatment in a late-stage trial, pharmaceutical partners Merck and Moderna announced Thursday.

The results are a significant advance in melanoma treatment; Merck and Moderna are already engaging with regulatory authorities to get the vaccine approved. More broadly, the vaccine's success bolsters hope that mRNA vaccines and individualized cancer-targeting medicines offer a new paradigm for cancer therapies.

"From the beginning, we believed mRNA technology could make it possible to develop cancer treatments uniquely matched to each patient’s tumor," Moderna CEO Stéphane Bancel said in a statement. The trial results "mark a defining moment for that vision and show what’s possible when we rethink how medicines are made." They also "reinforce our conviction that mRNA has the potential to fundamentally change how we treat cancer," he said.

Government officials in Guinea-Bissau have formally rejected a clinical trial that would be run by Danish researchers and funded by the US to look for alleged nonspecific harms from the newborn dose of the hepatitis B vaccine. The vaccine dose is currently recommended globally for all newborns and has been used safely and effectively for decades.

In an email to Ars Technica, one of the trial's lead researchers, Christine Stabell Benn of Bandim Health Project and the University of Southern Denmark, said that Guinea-Bissau's Council of Ministers "formally reaffirmed its non-acceptance of the study on 16 October."

It hasn’t even been a month since its launch, but the wonderfully hectic CONTROL Resonant has already sold its first million copies, Remedy Entertainment announced today. The game has surpassed Alan Wake 2 as the developer’s fastest-selling game.

CONTROL Resonant received plenty of praise from critics and fans alike, including a nine out of 10 in our review. After putting nearly 90 hours into the game, it’s not all that surprising to see it thrive, but it only took Resonant three weeks to clear the milestone. It comfortably dethroned Alan Wake 2, which needed around two months to reach those figures.

Remedy carved a spot in fans’ hearts after releasing a series of incredible games, such as Alan Wake, Quantum Break, and CONTROL. Despite the game leaking a day early, internet pirates pledged their support for the studio, asking people to buy the game rather than download it. It’s hard to think of another studio that could instill that sort of fondness.

It’s not entirely fair to compare sales figures between CONTROL Resonant and Alan Wake 2, however. The previous champion is exclusive to the Epic Games Store, which was already quite reviled by gamers.

Not putting the game on Steam meant a smaller market, and that’s before Epic’s latest controversy. In the meantime, Epic proudly announced a vibe-coded version of its store, bragging about it being all of five seconds faster than Steam—only to get outpaced by its rival before the new store was even live.

CONTROL Resonant is a masterful follow-up to the critically acclaimed CONTROL, picking up from where its older sibling left off. You play as Dylan Faden, the brother of Jesse Faden. Jesse was the protagonist in the original game, introducing fans to the paranatural mysteries of the Federal Bureau of Control.

Resonant plays wildly differently than its predecessor, bringing a high-octane hack-and-slash to the streets of New York. If you played the first title, you’ve already seen Jesse’s share of the story; now it’s time to experience Dylan’s journey. And while Remedy maintains you don’t necessarily need to play the first game, it does make the story a lot better, in our opinion.

CONTROL Resonant is bound to sell even more as the years go by. Remedy promised a slew of improvements, including the upcoming Photo Mode, and the studio already addressed pain points such as early-game difficulty and limited number of runs. The game will likely have DLC as well, which will surely spark new interest in the game.

Ubisoft is shutting down this forgotten Rainbow Six game you probably didn’t even know existed

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Rainbow Six franchise has a wealth of installments, and when it comes to this amount of volume, you can’t always get them right. That’s the case for Rainbow Six Mobile, which is shutting down in January 2027, Ubisoft announced today.

The game will stay up until Jan. 15, 2027, the developer said, and its upcoming season “will be released as planned next week.” The announcement teases more updates to prepare for the transition, though it’s unclear what this means in practice.

Rainbow Six Mobile has had an unusual run since its reveal in 2022. At the time, Ubisoft said it was a version of Rainbow Six Siege built from the ground up to “adapt the Siege experience to mobile devices.”

Rainbow Six Extraction, the other forgotten R6 spinoff. Image via Ubisoft

Despite a few closed tests and soft launches after 2023 and a litany of changes in 2024, the game only released globally in February 2026. It has had less than eight months in the hands of players all over the world before being sunset—which, as we all know by now, is the “your dog went to live on a farm” of the gaming industry.

At first glance, Rainbow Six Mobile may almost seem doomed to fail. Siege‘s identity is built on its tactical, unforgiving gameplay—the kind that may get you to hold an angle with an ACOG on your gun, so tensely you can barely breathe. It’s a hard element to bring to a mobile game.

“Our goal is not to have you stop playing on PC or console and switch to mobile,” the team said on the announcement blog post. “We know there is nothing like the adrenaline rush of a close Siege match on the big screen or the precision of a mouse and keyboard.” Instead, the game aimed to let fans “get the R6 experience” in “short, accessible bursts,” the developers said.

It’s unclear what will happen to Rainbow Six Mobile until its shutdown date, but Ubisoft will announce more of its plans “in the coming weeks,” according to its announcement.

CONTROL Resonant is pretty stellar on its own, but the game will get even better in the next couple of months. Remedy is adding the coveted Photo Mode in November, plus adding a “New Game ++” next month, the studio announced today.

CONTROL Resonant has some of the most breathtaking environments in Remedy history, with altered Manhattan and the Unknown Zone being particularly scenic. Though the game released recently, fans are already vehemently asking for a Photo Mode, and the developer listened.

“The Photo Mode for CONTROL Resonant is something we’ve been cooking for a while,” Remedy said in a blog post today. “We even consulted some talented virtual photographers from the community to help us make it the best it can be.”

Some tricks are good, but not fail-proof. Screenshot by Destructoid

Despite being a beloved feature from the first CONTROL, Photo Mode wasn’t available in Resonant at launch “because we needed to prioritize making sure the game was in the best possible shape,” the post adds, but the feature is scheduled for “sometime in November.”

In the meantime, players can get a faux Photo Mode by getting Dylan to face a wall and rotating the camera around him. Done from the right angle, he disappears from the frame, but this is an imperfect solution that doesn’t work everywhere.

Though highly anticipated, the addition of Photo Mode isn’t entirely surprising for the community. CONTROL also added the feature after launch, and given how much work Remedy poured into the environments in the sequel, allowing fans to screenshot to their heart’s content is a natural choice.

Before Photo Mode hits, Remedy is also making a big improvement to the New Game Plus experience by letting you replay it as often as you like. As it stands, you only get the first playthrough and an encore, after which you’re stuck with the large (but finite) amount of side quests and secrets.

Screenshot by Destructoid

With the new addition, however, you can run as many subsequent playthroughs as you want, offering you more ways to progress through the game’s multiple zones. You also get the chance to revisit the main story and side quests, opting for different dialogue if you missed it the first time around. Those parallel stories often have multiple endings, so you may want to replay them more than once or twice. “New Game ++” is scheduled to launch in October.

CONTROL Resonant shot up to the top of Steam’s best-selling list when it released yesterday, marking another successful entry into the Remedyverse. We gave it a valiant nine out of 10 for its fluid combat, stunning environments, and delightful exploration, which are bound to become even better after the studio adds these two features.

Reacher star Alan Ritchson has claimed that a white supremacist storyline for the show was scrapped after Donald Trump was re-elected.

The Amazon Prime Video series is based on Lee Child’s Jack Reacher novels, and features Ritchson as the central character.

In a recent social media interaction with a fan, Ritchson said that they had attempted to adapt Die Trying, the 1998 book in the series, “where he kills his way through a white supremacist militia camp”.

Writing on Threads, the actor alluded to Trump’s 2024 presidential victory as he said: “My favourite book. Fun fact: I had it approved until someone got re-elected.”

Last year, the actor – who is a Christian – opened up about the criticism that puzzles him, telling GQ: “People have this weird relationship with fictional characters, and sort of equate that to the real human being.

“I don’t have that problem, making that distinction. I get criticized a lot by Christians who are like, ‘How dare you have an unmarried sex scene on TV and then talk about Jesus?’ I’m sorry—I doubt that’s going to be part of the conversation when I’m standing at the pearly gates!”

Reacher recently aired its fourth season, which is based on Child’s 2009 novel Gone Tomorrow, while the show recently debuted spin-off show Neagley, focused on Maria Sten’s character Frances Neagley.

In other news, Nicki Minaj has defended her support of Trump after becoming an advocate for the US Republican Party over the past year.

“I am honoured to be able to have an opportunity to work with this administration and to work with President Trump,” she said on Fox News’ The Ingraham Angle. “He has inspired many people, not just me. And he’s – he’s fun.”

“He’s not just smart, and, you know, he’s not just the president,” she continued. “But he’s also someone that I can relate to, someone that, you know, every time I see him, or every time I see him speaking to other people, he’s just himself. He’s very… he’s very grounded. It’s easy to connect with him. It’s easy to feel like he heard you; he understands you. And I just… I just love him. And I’m not going to ever make excuses for that.”

When host Ingraham asked the rapper if she had anything to “say to the critics of you for working with the sitting US President”, Minaj responded: “Absolutely nothing. I don’t know why I should.”

Art Garfunkel says Donald Trump and global warming are making him want to “leave” America

Art Garfunkel has admitted that he is considering “leaving America” due to global warming and Donald Trump.

  • READ MORE: Art Garfunkel tells us about reconciling with Paul Simon: “I burst into tears – and then there were hugs”

Speaking in a new interview with The Guardian, Garfunkel expressed his dismay over the current administration in the United States under President Trump.

“I don’t like America’s position at all. I’m not aboard, and I’m thinking of leaving the country, frankly. It’s real that the planet is getting warmer, that the water’s rising, that so many people are starving,” he said.

“When Donald Trump took over Venezuela, are those the new rules? Just take over a country and run it? It’s shocking, and you think: how on earth are we gonna change the presidency?”

His comments come eight months after US forces captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro in Caracas and brought him to the United States to face federal charges.

Despite Garfunkel’s pessimism he added: “Trump won’t last forever. So we’ve got to hope.”

Garfunkel is not the only celebrity who has threatened to quit the US because of Trump.

Previously, Avatar director James Cameron said he was “sickened” by Trump’s re-election in 2024 and admitted the he was happy his New Zealand citizenship was “imminent”.

“I see a turn away from everything decent,” he said at the time. “America doesn’t stand for anything if it doesn’t stand for what it has historically stood for. It becomes a hollow idea, and I think they’re hollowing it out as fast as they can for their own benefit.”

Comedian and TV host Rosie O’Donnell also moved to Ireland when Trump was re-elected.

“I miss many things about life there at home and I’m trying to find a home here in this beautiful country and when it is safe for all citizens to have equal rights there in America, that’s when we will consider coming back,” she said at the time. “It’s been heartbreaking to see what’s happening politically and hard for me personally as well. The personal is political, as we all know”.

Her comments prompted Trump to threaten to remove her US citizenship.

Meanwhile, Art Garfunkel recently opened up to NME about ending “years of detachment” from Paul Simon.

Simon & Garfunkel were one of the best-selling acts of the 1960s, selling over 100 million records and recording timeless hits such as ‘Bridge Over Troubled Water’, ‘The Sound of Silence’ and ‘Mrs. Robinson’.

However, they also became known for their troubled relationship, with artistic disagreements leading to their breakup in 1970. They have performed together sporadically since then, but not since 2010.

The latest feud followed a particularly razor-tongued 2015 interview Garfunkel gave to The Telegraph, where the singer labelled his former musical cohort a “jerk” and an “idiot” – an outburst he said he subsequently regretted.

Speaking about a recent meeting with Simon, he told NME: “Paul said to me, ‘Arty, it’s not that you spoke to the British press and that you didn’t do it well…[it’s that] I felt you wanted to hurt me. That’s what got me’. And I thought, ‘That’s true’. I wanted to hurt him. The next thing I knew, I burst into tears… and then there were hugs.”

Johnny Marr has reacted to Prime Minister Andy Burnham covering The Smiths on guitar during a visit to Kyiv.

  • READ MORE: Government announce £45million to “turn up” music industry for artists, businesses and children – but what do Andy Burnham’s government need to do now?

The PM, who previously served as Mayor of Greater Manchester, jammed ‘Rusholme Ruffians’ from the band’s 1985 album ‘Meat Is Murder’ alongside a Ukrainian soldier.

“I’m out of practice, a little bit,” he said as he strummed the instrument. He also explained: “It’s a song by a Manchester band called The Smiths.”

Burnham’s first visit to Ukraine as PM saw him go to the country’s military and artistic unit, who perform on the front lines, in hospitals and at rehabilitation centres.

During a new interview with Absolute Radio, Marr was asked about the PM covering The Smiths. “I’ve only just been told about it, in great detail,” he responded.

“I think it’s an amazing thing. In all honesty, for me, it’s like a victory for the guitar. [It’s] just the way I’m wired; I always think like that.”

He continued: “I can’t quite get my head around it. I don’t know whether pride is the word, but it probably is. It’s kind of beyond me to sort of process it, really. Because it could be massive that, but I just sort of go, ‘How great is the guitar?’ What an amazing tool the guitar is, you know?

“My overwhelming feeling was that it was a victory of the guitar, really. So I know that this being political – and obviously with the plight of Ukraine… The more I think about it, the more I think how insignificant I am in it, really.”

Marr then agreed that the nod from Burnham shows the importance of music, globally, adding: “That’s a nice thought – I absolutely agree. And good on him, you know?” Check out a clip of the interview above.

Burnham called The Smiths’ 1984 compilation ‘Hatful Of Hollow’ “the record that changed everything” for him, and has previously hailed it as one of his favourite albums of all time (via The Quietus).

Last month, he featured The Smiths in a blind-ranking of his favourite British bands, along with the likes of Oasis, Elbow and Wolf Alice. Earlier this year, Oasis seemingly endorsed Burnham by permitting him to use ‘Some Might Say’ in a by-election campaign video.

Shortly after becoming Prime Minister, Burnham announced that music venues, clubs and pubs in England would get a 20 per cent cut in business rates next year. The costs will be funded following a review of the tax reliefs given to companies “such as vape shops”.

Elsewhere, Radiohead’s Thom Yorke, members of Massive Attack, and Robert Smith of The Cure are among the 200 major artists calling on Burnham’s government to block the proposed development of the controversial new Rosebank oil field in the North Sea.

Johnny Marr. CREDIT: Andy Cotterill

There is now pressure on the PM to remove expensive, prohibitive barriers of the post-Brexit touring crisis, crack down on the copyright exceptions in relation to AI, and make good on the government’s previous promises to ban ticket touts.

This spring, Burnham helped confirm that Manchester festival Beyond The Music – which he helped found – would be returning in 2026. He is set to participate in this year’s edition in October.

He spoke to NME along with other figures from the music industry in 2024 about the event, and highlighted the dire need to support grassroots music venues and new artists across the UK.

NME asked Burnham for his reaction to Coldplay making their own ticket levy, after the band announced they would donate 10 per cent of their profits from their 2025 UK stadium shows to save the grassroots.

With the move, Chris Martin and co. followed in the footsteps of Enter Shikari, and it came in the wake of recommendations from MPs to add a £1 levy on arena and stadium gigs.

“I wholeheartedly support it,” Burnham told NME at the time. “I made a call here last year that the industry needs to do more to give back to support its own grassroots venues, rehearsal spaces and talent development systems. I think it’s brilliant, and I applaud Coldplay for doing that.”

In other news, Johnny Marr has shared a new single, ‘It’s Time’, which sees him take on “assholes with dodgy views”. The song will appear on the singer-songwriter’s fifth solo album ‘The Age Of Everything’, out this autumn.

He played a big homecoming gig at Manchester’s Castlefield Bowl this summer, ahead of a stop at Dublin’s Iveagh Gardens. Marr’s concert at London’s OVO Arena Wembley, initially scheduled for October 24, was postponed last month. A new date is yet to be confirmed.
